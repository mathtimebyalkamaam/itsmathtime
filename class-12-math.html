Chapter 1 Unit I

Relations & Functions

Types of relations: reflexive ($(a, a) \in R$), symmetric ($(a, b) \in R \implies (b, a) \in R$), transitive ($(a, b), (b, c) \in R \implies (a, c) \in R$), equivalence relations. One-one (injective) and onto (surjective) functions, bijective functions.

• Injectivity Test: $f(x_1) = f(x_2) \implies x_1 = x_2$
• Surjectivity Test: For every $y \in \text{Codomain}$, $\exists x \in \text{Domain}$ such that $f(x) = y$
💡 Always prove all three conditions explicitly to score full 3 marks on equivalence relation questions.
Chapter 2 Unit I

Inverse Trigonometric Functions

Definition, range, domain, principal value branch. Graphs of inverse trigonometric functions and fundamental evaluation rules.

• $\sin^{-1} x$: Domain $[-1, 1]$, Principal Branch $\left[ -\frac{\pi}{2}, \frac{\pi}{2} \right]$
• $\cos^{-1} x$: Domain $[-1, 1]$, Principal Branch $[0, \pi]$
• $\tan^{-1} x$: Domain $\mathbb{R}$, Principal Branch $\left( -\frac{\pi}{2}, \frac{\pi}{2} \right)$
⚠️ Exam Trap: $\cos^{-1}\left(\cos\frac{7\pi}{6}\right) \neq \frac{7\pi}{6}$ because $\frac{7\pi}{6} \notin [0, \pi]$. Write $\cos\left(2\pi - \frac{5\pi}{6}\right) = \cos\frac{5\pi}{6} \implies \text{Answer: } \frac{5\pi}{6}$.
Chapter 3 Unit II

Matrices

Matrix notation, order, types, zero and identity matrix, transpose of a matrix, symmetric and skew-symmetric matrices. Operations on matrices (addition, scalar multiplication, multiplication).

• Non-Commutative: $AB \neq BA$ in general
• Symmetric Decomposition: $A = \frac{1}{2}(A + A^T) + \frac{1}{2}(A - A^T)$
• Transpose of Product: $(AB)^T = B^T A^T$ (Reversal Law)
Diagonal elements of any skew-symmetric matrix are always identically zero.
Chapter 4 • Guaranteed LA Unit II

Determinants

Determinant of a square matrix (up to $3 \times 3$), minors, cofactors, adjoint and inverse of a matrix. Solving systems of linear equations in 2 or 3 variables using matrix inversion method.

• Matrix Inverse: $A^{-1} = \frac{1}{|A|} \text{adj}(A) \quad (\text{provided } |A| \neq 0)$
• System Solution: $AX = B \implies X = A^{-1} B$
• Determinant of Adjoint: $|\text{adj}(A)| = |A|^{n-1}$
🎯 Always verify that $|A| \neq 0$ before computing cofactors to secure the first step mark in the 5-mark matrix method question.
Chapter 5 • Calculus Core Unit III

Continuity & Differentiability

Continuity at a point and in an interval, derivative of composite functions (Chain Rule), implicit differentiation, logarithmic differentiation, derivatives of functions in parametric form, second-order derivatives.

• Continuity Condition: $\lim_{x \to c^-} f(x) = \lim_{x \to c^+} f(x) = f(c)$
• Parametric Derivative: $\frac{dy}{dx} = \frac{dy/dt}{dx/dt}$, $\frac{d^2 y}{dx^2} = \frac{d}{dt}\left(\frac{dy}{dx}\right) \cdot \frac{dt}{dx}$
• Logarithmic Differentiation: $y = [u(x)]^{v(x)} \implies \ln y = v(x) \ln u(x)$
⚠️ Critical Pitfall: Forgetting $\frac{dt}{dx}$ when evaluating second derivatives of parametric functions is the #1 mark-loser in Section C.
Chapter 6 Unit III

Applications of Derivatives

Rate of change of bodies, increasing and decreasing functions on intervals, maxima and minima (first and second derivative tests), real-world optimization problems (frequently featured in Section E Case Studies).

• Strictly Increasing: $f'(x) > 0$; Strictly Decreasing: $f'(x) < 0$
• Local Maxima: $f'(c) = 0$ and $f''(c) < 0$
• Local Minima: $f'(c) = 0$ and $f''(c) > 0$
Always state the open interval explicitly when writing increasing/decreasing solutions.
Chapter 7 • The Giant Unit III

Integrals

Integration as inverse process of differentiation. Methods: substitution, partial fractions, integration by parts. Definite integrals and properties (King's Rule, symmetry for even/odd functions).

• By Parts: $\int u v \, dx = u \int v \, dx - \int \left( u' \int v \, dx \right) dx$
• King's Property: $\int_0^a f(x) \, dx = \int_0^a f(a - x) \, dx$
• Special Integral: $\int e^x [f(x) + f'(x)] \, dx = e^x f(x) + C$

Chapter 8 Unit III

Applications of the Integrals

Area under simple curves, especially lines, circles, parabolas, and ellipses in standard form. Setting up vertical or horizontal integration strips and finding points of intersection.

• Vertical Strips Formula: $\text{Area} = \int_a^b y \, dx = \int_a^b f(x) \, dx$
• Horizontal Strips Formula: $\text{Area} = \int_c^d x \, dy = \int_c^d g(y) \, dy$
⚠️ A neat, shaded sketch with intersection points labeled is mandatory. CBSE marking schemes assign 1 full mark to the diagram alone.
Chapter 9 Unit III

Differential Equations

Order and degree of differential equations, general and particular solutions, solution of differential equations by method of separation of variables, homogeneous differential equations, and linear first-order differential equations.

• Linear Form: $\frac{dy}{dx} + Py = Q \implies \text{IF} = e^{\int P \, dx}$
• General Solution: $y \cdot (\text{IF}) = \int [Q \cdot (\text{IF})] \, dx + C$
• Homogeneous Substitution: $y = vx \implies \frac{dy}{dx} = v + x \frac{dv}{dx}$
Degree is defined only when the differential equation is a polynomial in its derivatives.
Chapter 10 • Vectors Unit IV

Vector Algebra

Vectors and scalars, magnitude and direction, direction cosines and direction ratios, unit vectors, position vector, scalar (dot) product and vector (cross) product of vectors with geometric applications.

• Dot Product: $\vec{a} \cdot \vec{b} = |\vec{a}| |\vec{b}| \cos \theta \implies \cos \theta = \frac{\vec{a} \cdot \vec{b}}{|\vec{a}| |\vec{b}|}$
• Cross Product: $\vec{a} \times \vec{b} = |\vec{a}| |\vec{b}| \sin \theta \, \hat{n}$
• Projection of $\vec{a}$ on $\vec{b}$: $\frac{\vec{a} \cdot \vec{b}}{|\vec{b}|}$
Perpendicular condition: $\vec{a} \cdot \vec{b} = 0$ Launch 3D Vector Lab →
Chapter 11 • 5M Pillar Unit IV

Three-Dimensional Geometry

Direction cosines ($l^2 + m^2 + n^2 = 1$) and direction ratios ($a, b, c$), Cartesian and vector equations of a line passing through a point and parallel to a vector, or through two points. Shortest distance between skew lines.

• Vector Line: $\vec{r} = \vec{a} + \lambda \vec{b}$
• Cartesian Line: $\frac{x - x_1}{a} = \frac{y - y_1}{b} = \frac{z - z_1}{c}$
• Skew Lines Distance: $d = \left| \frac{(\vec{a}_2 - \vec{a}_1) \cdot (\vec{b}_1 \times \vec{b}_2)}{|\vec{b}_1 \times \vec{b}_2|} \right|$
🎯 The skew lines distance question is a guaranteed 5-mark question in Section D.
Chapter 12 • 100% Scoring Unit V

Linear Programming (LPP)

Mathematical formulation of LPP, objective function $Z = ax + by$, constraints, feasible region, bounded and unbounded feasible regions, corner point method for finding optimal solutions.

• Corner Point Method: Evaluate $Z$ at each vertex of the feasible region.
• Unbounded Region Rule: Check open half-plane $ax + by > M$ (for max) or $< m$ (for min).
💡 Always draw clean axis scales and test $(0, 0)$ to verify correct shading direction.
Chapter 13 • High-Yield Unit VI

Probability

Conditional probability $P(A|B) = \frac{P(A \cap B)}{P(B)}$, multiplication rule of probability, independent events ($P(A \cap B) = P(A)P(B)$), total probability theorem, and Bayes' theorem.

• Law of Total Probability:
$P(A) = \sum_{j=1}^n P(E_j) P(A | E_j)$
• Bayes' Theorem:
$P(E_i | A) = \frac{P(E_i) P(A | E_i)}{\sum_{j=1}^n P(E_j) P(A | E_j)}$
🎯 Clearly state events $E_1, E_2, \dots$ and target event $A$ in prose before writing probabilities to secure full presentation marks.

Three Canonical Class 12 Board Examination Derivations

Step-by-step master proofs written in pure mathematical LaTeX according to the official CBSE Class 12 marking scheme.

Canonical Board Proof 1 Section D Pillar (Calculus)

Canonical Derivation of Integration by Parts & The ILATE Rule

Origin: The Integration by Parts formula is the exact integral analog of the Product Rule of Differential Calculus.

Step 1: Start with the product rule of derivatives for two differentiable functions $u$ and $w$:
$\frac{d}{dx}[u \cdot w] = u \frac{dw}{dx} + w \frac{du}{dx}$
Step 2: Let $w = \int v \, dx$, which directly implies that $\frac{dw}{dx} = v$:
$\frac{d}{dx}\left[ u \int v \, dx \right] = u v + \left( \int v \, dx \right) \frac{du}{dx}$
Step 3: Integrate both sides with respect to $x$:
$\int \frac{d}{dx}\left[ u \int v \, dx \right] dx = \int u v \, dx + \int \left[ \frac{du}{dx} \left( \int v \, dx \right) \right] dx$
Step 4: By the Fundamental Theorem of Calculus, the left side simplifies:
$u \int v \, dx = \int u v \, dx + \int \left( \frac{du}{dx} \int v \, dx \right) dx$
Step 5: Rearrange to obtain the canonical Integration by Parts formula:
$\int u v \, dx = u \int v \, dx - \int \left( \frac{du}{dx} \int v \, dx \right) dx \quad \text{(Derived)}$
ILATE Function Priority: First function $u$ is chosen by priority order: Inverse Trig $\to$ Logarithmic $\to$ Algebraic $\to$ Trigonometric $\to$ Exponential.
Canonical Board Proof 2 Section D / E Pillar (Probability)

Derivation of Bayes' Theorem from the Law of Total Probability

Setup: Let $\{E_1, E_2, \dots, E_n\}$ form a partition of the sample space $S$ (mutually exclusive: $E_i \cap E_j = \emptyset$ for $i \neq j$, and exhaustive: $\bigcup_{j=1}^n E_j = S$), with each $P(E_j) > 0$. Let $A$ be any non-zero event associated with $S$.

Step 1: Express event $A$ in terms of partition intersections:
Since $S = E_1 \cup E_2 \cup \dots \cup E_n$, we can write:
$A = A \cap S = A \cap \left( \bigcup_{j=1}^n E_j \right) = \bigcup_{j=1}^n (A \cap E_j)$
Step 2: Apply the addition axiom on pairwise disjoint sets $(A \cap E_j)$:
$P(A) = \sum_{j=1}^n P(A \cap E_j)$
Step 3: Apply the Multiplication Rule of Probability $P(A \cap E_j) = P(E_j) P(A | E_j)$:
$P(A) = \sum_{j=1}^n P(E_j) P(A | E_j) \quad \text{(Law of Total Probability)}$
Step 4: Use the definition of conditional probability for posterior probability $P(E_i | A)$:
$P(E_i | A) = \frac{P(E_i \cap A)}{P(A)} = \frac{P(E_i) P(A | E_i)}{P(A)}$
Step 5: Substitute $P(A)$ from the Law of Total Probability:
$P(E_i | A) = \frac{P(E_i) P(A | E_i)}{\sum_{j=1}^n P(E_j) P(A | E_j)} \quad \text{(Q.E.D.)}$
Canonical Board Proof 3 5-Mark Standard Theory Proof (3D)

Shortest Distance Between Two Skew Lines in 3D Space

Setup: Let two non-intersecting, non-parallel lines in 3D space be: $L_1: \vec{r} = \vec{a}_1 + \lambda \vec{b}_1$ (passing through point $A(\vec{a}_1)$ parallel to $\vec{b}_1$) and $L_2: \vec{r} = \vec{a}_2 + \mu \vec{b}_2$ (passing through point $B(\vec{a}_2)$ parallel to $\vec{b}_2$).

Step 1: Determine the common normal vector perpendicular to both lines:
The vector $\vec{n} = \vec{b}_1 \times \vec{b}_2$ is perpendicular to both $\vec{b}_1$ and $\vec{b}_2$. Thus, the unit vector along the line of shortest distance is:
$\hat{n} = \frac{\vec{b}_1 \times \vec{b}_2}{|\vec{b}_1 \times \vec{b}_2|}$
Step 2: Connect points $A$ and $B$ on the two lines:
The vector joining arbitrary points $A(\vec{a}_1)$ on $L_1$ and $B(\vec{a}_2)$ on $L_2$ is $\vec{AB} = \vec{a}_2 - \vec{a}_1$.
Step 3: The shortest distance $d$ is the magnitude of the projection of $\vec{AB}$ along $\hat{n}$:
$d = |\text{Projection of } \vec{AB} \text{ on } \hat{n}| = |(\vec{a}_2 - \vec{a}_1) \cdot \hat{n}|$
Step 4: Substitute unit vector $\hat{n}$:
$d = \left| \frac{(\vec{a}_2 - \vec{a}_1) \cdot (\vec{b}_1 \times \vec{b}_2)}{|\vec{b}_1 \times \vec{b}_2|} \right| \quad \text{(Derived)}$
Coplanarity Condition: If the two lines intersect (are coplanar), the shortest distance is $0 \implies (\vec{a}_2 - \vec{a}_1) \cdot (\vec{b}_1 \times \vec{b}_2) = 0$.

Board Exam Strategy

Alka Ma'am's 5 Golden Rules for 95%+ in Class 12 Boards

Classroom-tested exam psychology and marking scheme tactics from 20+ years of CBSE evaluation:

01

Master the 3-Pass Exam Execution Protocol

Never solve the paper sequentially when stuck. Pass 1 (first 90 mins): secure all guaranteed questions in Section A, B, and standard Calculus. Pass 2 (50 mins): tackle 5-mark proofs and Case Studies. Pass 3 (40 mins): revise calculations, units, and constant $+ C$.

02

The Constant $+ C$ & Non-Singular Condition Guard

Evaluators deduct $\frac{1}{2}$ mark every single time you omit $+ C$ in indefinite integrals. Similarly, in matrix inversion questions, writing $|A| \neq 0 \implies A^{-1} \text{ exists}$ is a mandatory 1-mark step before calculating the cofactor matrix.

03

Shaded Diagram Is Mandatory in AOI

In Chapter 8 (Applications of Integrals), evaluating $\int_a^b (y_1 - y_2) \, dx$ without a sketch forfeits 1 full mark. Draw the curve, label the axes and intersection points, shade the target bounded area, and indicate the representative strip clearly.

04

Define Events in Prose for Bayes' Theorem

In Section D or E Probability questions, never write raw fraction numbers immediately. Always write: "Let $E_1$ = event that coin is biased, $E_2$ = event that coin is fair, $A$ = event of obtaining heads". Explicit event definitions earn 1 free mark.

05

Solve 5 Official CBSE SQPs Under Strict 3-Hour Clock

Knowledge without speed causes panic in Section D and E. Solve at least 5 previous years' official CBSE question papers from 10:30 AM to 1:30 PM (matching the exact board exam slot) without touching your phone. Exam temperament is built through simulation.

Frequently Asked Questions: CBSE Class 12 Math

Clear, pedagogical answers to common student challenges and board examination strategies.

Which unit carries the highest weightage in CBSE Class 12 Math Board Exam?

Unit III (Calculus) carries an overwhelming 35 Marks out of the 80-mark theoretical examination (43.75%). It encompasses Continuity and Differentiability, Applications of Derivatives, Integrals, Applications of Integrals, and Differential Equations.

How is the 80-mark CBSE Class 12 Math theory question paper structured?

The paper contains 5 sections: Section A (20 MCQs & Assertion-Reason, 1M each), Section B (5 VSA, 2M each), Section C (6 SA, 3M each), Section D (4 Long Answer, 5M each), and Section E (3 Case-Based Questions, 4M each with internal sub-parts).

What is the formula for the shortest distance between two skew lines in vector form?

For skew lines $\vec{r} = \vec{a}_1 + \lambda \vec{b}_1$ and $\vec{r} = \vec{a}_2 + \mu \vec{b}_2$, the shortest distance $d$ is: $$d = \left| \frac{(\vec{a}_2 - \vec{a}_1) \cdot (\vec{b}_1 \times \vec{b}_2)}{|\vec{b}_1 \times \vec{b}_2|} \right|$$
