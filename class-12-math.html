NCERT Theorem 11.2

Shortest Distance Between Two Skew Lines in 3D Space

★ 100% High-Frequency 5-Mark Proof
Statement: Let two skew lines $L_1$ and $L_2$ be given by $\vec{r} = \vec{a}_1 + \lambda \vec{b}_1$ and $\vec{r} = \vec{a}_2 + \mu \vec{b}_2$. Prove that the shortest distance $d$ between them is given by:
$d = \left| \frac{(\vec{b}_1 \times \vec{b}_2) \cdot (\vec{a}_2 - \vec{a}_1)}{|\vec{b}_1 \times \vec{b}_2|} \right|$.

Given Equations & Normal Vector:

• $L_1$ passes through point $A(\vec{a}_1)$ parallel to vector $\vec{b}_1$.

• $L_2$ passes through point $B(\vec{a}_2)$ parallel to vector $\vec{b}_2$.

• Vector $\vec{n} = \vec{b}_1 \times \vec{b}_2$ is perpendicular to both lines.

Proof Steps via Projection:

1. Let $PQ$ be the shortest distance line segment, which lies along the common perpendicular unit vector $\hat{n} = \frac{\vec{b}_1 \times \vec{b}_2}{|\vec{b}_1 \times \vec{b}_2|}$.

2. Vector connecting known points on lines: $\vec{AB} = \vec{a}_2 - \vec{a}_1$.

3. Shortest distance $d$ is the magnitude of the projection of $\vec{AB}$ on unit normal $\hat{n}$.

4. $d = |\vec{AB} \cdot \hat{n}| = \left| \frac{(\vec{b}_1 \times \vec{b}_2) \cdot (\vec{a}_2 - \vec{a}_1)}{|\vec{b}_1 \times \vec{b}_2|} \right|$. (Proved)

Direct Answer Key: If $(\vec{b}_1 \times \vec{b}_2) \cdot (\vec{a}_2 - \vec{a}_1) = 0$, then lines $L_1$ and $L_2$ intersect (coplanar). Inspect 3D Skew Lines Live in Simulator →
NCERT Theorem 7.1

Integration by Parts Formula

Essential Calculus Foundation
Statement: For any two differentiable functions $u(x)$ and $v(x)$, prove that:
$\int u(x) v(x) \, dx = u(x) \int v(x) \, dx - \int \left( \frac{du}{dx} \int v(x) \, dx \right) dx$.

Product Rule of Differentiation:

Let $f(x) = u(x) w(x)$, where $w(x) = \int v(x) \, dx$, meaning $\frac{dw}{dx} = v(x)$.

$\frac{d}{dx}(u \cdot w) = u \frac{dw}{dx} + w \frac{du}{dx} = u(x) v(x) + w(x) \frac{du}{dx}$.

Integration on Both Sides:

1. Integrate both sides with respect to $x$:

2. $u(x) w(x) = \int u(x) v(x) \, dx + \int \left( \frac{du}{dx} w(x) \right) dx$.

3. Rearranging for $\int u(x) v(x) \, dx$ and replacing $w(x) = \int v(x) \, dx$:

4. $\int u v \, dx = u \int v \, dx - \int \left( u' \int v \, dx \right) dx$. (Proved)

Direct Takeaway: Select first function $u$ using the ILATE rule (Inverse, Logarithmic, Algebraic, Trigonometric, Exponential). Accumulate Integrals in 3D Lab →
NCERT Theorem 13.2

Bayes' Theorem for Total Probability Partition

★ 5-Mark Guaranteed Question
Statement: If $E_1, E_2, \dots, E_n$ form a partition of sample space $S$ (mutually exclusive and exhaustive with $P(E_i) > 0$), and $A$ is any event with $P(A) > 0$, then:
$P(E_i|A) = \frac{P(E_i) P(A|E_i)}{\sum_{j=1}^n P(E_j) P(A|E_j)}$.

Definition of Conditional Probability:

$P(E_i|A) = \frac{P(E_i \cap A)}{P(A)}$

Multiplication Theorem:

$P(E_i \cap A) = P(E_i) P(A|E_i)$

Theorem of Total Probability:

1. Since $E_1, \dots, E_n$ partition $S$, $A = (A \cap E_1) \cup (A \cap E_2) \cup \dots \cup (A \cap E_n)$.

2. $P(A) = \sum_{j=1}^n P(A \cap E_j) = \sum_{j=1}^n P(E_j) P(A|E_j)$.

3. Substituting $P(E_i \cap A)$ and $P(A)$ into the conditional probability formula:

4. $P(E_i|A) = \frac{P(E_i) P(A|E_i)}{\sum_{j=1}^n P(E_j) P(A|E_j)}$. (Proved)

Direct Answer Key: $P(E_i)$ are prior probabilities; $P(E_i|A)$ are posterior probabilities updated by observed evidence $A$. Simulate Probability Partitions in 3D →
NCERT Theorem 3.2

Uniqueness of the Inverse of a Square Matrix

Essential Algebra Proof
Statement: If $A$ is an invertible square matrix of order $n$, prove that its inverse is unique.

Hypothesis of Non-Uniqueness:

Suppose $A$ possesses two distinct inverses $B$ and $C$.

By definition of matrix inverse:

• $AB = BA = I$

• $AC = CA = I$

Associative Property Proof:

1. Consider the product $B(AC)$.

2. Since $AC = I$, $B(AC) = B \cdot I = B$.

3. By matrix multiplication associativity, $B(AC) = (BA)C$.

4. Since $BA = I$, $(BA)C = I \cdot C = C$.

5. Equating results: $B = C$. Hence, inverse of $A$ is unique. (Proved)

Alka Ma'am's 5 Golden Rules for 95%+ in Class 12 Math

✍️

1. State Every Integration Identity Explicitly

Never substitute integration constants or partial fractions silently. CBSE board examiners allocate distinct half-marks for writing King's Property or the ILATE formula before evaluating.

📐

2. Always Sketch Shaded Regions in Application of Integrals

For area bounded questions, an unlabelled graph costs 2 marks. Sketch the intersection points, mark whether you are slicing vertically ($dx$) or horizontally ($dy$), and establish integration limits cleanly.

⏱️

3. Write Vector Arrows Reliably on $\vec{a}$ and $\vec{b}$

In 3D geometry and vectors, omitting overhead arrows or writing dot product without a clear dot $(\cdot)$ causes examiner penalties. Keep scalar vs vector notation rigorous.

⚡

4. Protect 20 Minutes for Section E Case Studies

Class 12 case study questions test real-life applications of Maxima/Minima and Bayes' Theorem. Read the prompt thoroughly and write out known values into an organized parameter table first.

🎯

5. Use 3D Virtual Labs to Master Skew Lines

Three-dimensional geometry cannot be properly visualized on a 2D blackboard. Rotate the spatial axes in Alka Ma'am's virtual 3D labs to understand the common perpendicular distance instinctively.

Class 12 CBSE Math FAQs

Frequently Asked Board Exam Questions

Direct answers to the most common questions asked by Class 12 board aspirants and parents.

Which units carry the highest weightage in CBSE Class 12 Math?

Calculus carries the highest weightage at 35 marks (Continuity, Differentiability, Applications of Derivatives, Integrals, Applications of Integrals, and Differential Equations), followed by Vectors and 3D Geometry at 14 marks, Matrices & Determinants at 10 marks, Probability at 8 marks, Relations & Functions at 8 marks, and Linear Programming at 5 marks.

What is the official CBSE Class 12 Math question paper format?

The 80-mark theory paper is divided into 5 Sections: Section A (20 MCQs/Assertion-Reason, 1 mark each), Section B (5 Very Short Answer, 2 marks each), Section C (6 Short Answer, 3 marks each), Section D (4 Long Answer, 5 marks each), and Section E (3 Case-Based Source Studies, 4 marks each).

How do I secure 95%+ in CBSE Class 12 Mathematics?

Master every NCERT Miscellaneous exercise example, memorize canonical derivations (shortest distance between skew lines, integration by parts, Bayes' theorem), maintain strict step-marking discipline, and solve the last 5 years of CBSE board papers under strict 3-hour exam conditions.
