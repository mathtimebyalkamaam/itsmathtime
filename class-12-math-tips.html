CBSE Class 12 Math: Integration Shortcuts, Matrix Speed Hacks & 95%+ Board Tactics

Alka Sharma (Alka Ma'am)
Senior Mathematics Educator • M.Sc., B.Ed., CTET
• 11 Min Read • CBSE Class 12 Core Syllabus

Dear Class 12 warriors! Alka Ma'am here. Class 12 Mathematics carries a specific reputation: Calculus takes up 35 out of 80 marks, and if you manage integration well, your overall score crosses 90% almost effortlessly.

In this guide, there are no long lectures or abstract theoretical proofs. I am handing you the exact integration shortcuts, tabular by-parts methods, matrix adjoint tricks, and vector checklists that allow top scorers to complete the 3-hour CBSE board paper with 30 minutes left for step-verification. Let's master them one by one.

1

Definite Integrals: The "King's Property" ($P_4$) That Crushes 90% of Board Questions

Whenever you see a definite integral with symmetric limits $\int_0^{\pi/2}$ or $\int_a^b$ involving roots, trigonometric quotients, or $\ln(1 + \tan x)$, do not attempt indefinite integration! Apply the King's Property immediately:

$$\int_a^b f(x) \, dx = \int_a^b f(a + b - x) \, dx$$

Reflecting about $x = \frac{a+b}{2}$ preserves identical geometric area: $I + I = 2I = \int_a^b 1 \, dx$.

Classic Board Example:

$$I = \int_0^{\pi/2} \frac{\sqrt{\sin x}}{\sqrt{\sin x} + \sqrt{\cos x}} \, dx \quad \text{--- (1)}$$ $$I = \int_0^{\pi/2} \frac{\sqrt{\cos x}}{\sqrt{\cos x} + \sqrt{\sin x}} \, dx \quad \text{--- (2) [Applying King's Property]}$$

Adding (1) and (2):

$$2I = \int_0^{\pi/2} 1 \, dx = [x]_0^{\pi/2} = \frac{\pi}{2} \implies I = \frac{\pi}{4}$$

Solved in 4 lines! That is a guaranteed 3 or 4 marks.

2

The 10-Second $2 \times 2$ Matrix Inverse & Adjoint Hack

Never spend time computing all 4 cofactors ($C_{11}, C_{12}, C_{21}, C_{22}$) individually for a $2 \times 2$ matrix. Use this instant mental shortcut:

$$\text{If } A = \begin{pmatrix} a & b \\ c & d \end{pmatrix}, \quad \text{then } \text{adj}(A) = \begin{pmatrix} d & -b \\ -c & a \end{pmatrix}$$

Rule: Swap the main diagonal elements ($a \leftrightarrow d$), and change the signs of the off-diagonal elements ($-b, -c$)!

$$A^{-1} = \frac{1}{|A|} \text{adj}(A) = \frac{1}{ad - bc} \begin{pmatrix} d & -b \\ -c & a \end{pmatrix}$$

4

3D Geometry: The 5-Mark Skew Lines Guaranteed Step-Marking Template

The shortest distance between two skew lines $\vec{r} = \vec{a}_1 + \lambda \vec{b}_1$ and $\vec{r} = \vec{a}_2 + \mu \vec{b}_2$ appears in nearly every board exam. Ensure full marks by laying out your solution like this:

$$d = \left| \frac{(\vec{b}_1 \times \vec{b}_2) \cdot (\vec{a}_2 - \vec{a}_1)}{|\vec{b}_1 \times \vec{b}_2|} \right|$$
  1. Step 1: Identify and state $\vec{a}_1, \vec{b}_1, \vec{a}_2, \vec{b}_2$ cleanly (1 mark).
  2. Step 2: Calculate $\vec{a}_2 - \vec{a}_1$ (1 mark).
  3. Step 3: Compute the cross product $\vec{b}_1 \times \vec{b}_2$ via determinant (1.5 marks).
  4. Step 4: Find magnitude $|\vec{b}_1 \times \vec{b}_2|$ and take dot product (1.5 marks).
5

Linear Programming: The "Unbounded Region" Half-Plane Test

If your feasible region is unbounded (open to infinity), the smallest corner-point value $M$ is NOT automatically the minimum!

⚠️ Mandatory CBSE Step: You must graph the open half-plane $ax + by < M$. Only if this open half-plane has no common points with the feasible region can you declare $M$ as the minimum value. Forgetting this single verification costs 2 marks every single year!
